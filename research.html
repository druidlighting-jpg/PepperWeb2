What I Study

Research

I study the uncomfortable coexistence of capitalism and democracy—two systems with very different logics that must somehow share the same political space.

"Does capitalism dominate democracy, or vice versa?"

The Big Picture

My research focuses on what happens when corporate power meets democratic politics. I'm particularly interested in how large companies in finance and technology confront legislative and regulatory challenges—and why those confrontations sometimes become headline news while others remain invisible to voters.

The puzzle at the center of my work is this: democratic majorities often want policies that conflict with what powerful businesses prefer. Sometimes the public wins. More often, it doesn't. Understanding when and why matters enormously for the health of democracy.

Current Project

Banklash

The Feedback Loops of Financial Policymaking

European Research Council Advanced Grant · €2.5 million · Five years

Why did the 2008 financial crisis produce such different policy responses across countries? Banklash investigates the feedback loops connecting policymaking, public opinion, and media coverage. We're testing whether narratives about inequality—stories that frame it as systemic unfairness rather than individual failure—can shift public support for redistribution. Early findings show the United States as a fascinating outlier: American attitudes seem remarkably resistant to these framing effects.

Research Themes

Business Power

How corporations influence policy—not just through lobbying and campaign contributions, but through the "quiet politics" that happens when issues never reach public attention. Business power is often greatest when it's invisible.

Financial Regulation

From the 2008 crisis to cryptocurrency scandals, I study the politics of regulating finance. When do governments impose tough rules? When do they back off? Why does the same crisis produce different responses in different countries?

Corporate Governance

Who controls corporations? Shareholders, managers, stakeholders? I've studied how different countries answer this question—and how those answers have changed over time in Europe and Japan.

Media & Opinion

How does media coverage shape what citizens think about economic policy? When do people pay attention to issues of inequality and corporate power? When do they tune out—and why does that matter?

Questions I'm Working On

  • Under what conditions does public opinion triumph over interest group influence in economic regulation?
  • How do narratives about the causes of inequality influence public support for redistribution?
  • What explains cross-national variation in how governments regulate finance and technology?
  • Why do some policy battles become public fights while others stay confined to technical committees?
